July was the first proper month where I listened to albums in full. Despite only starting halfway through the month 
there was still plenty to be loved amongst the few I did manage to get through. Without further ado, here are the highlights.
<div class="paragraph-gradient ITAOTS">

#### Neutral Milk Hotel - In the Aeroplane Over the Sea

  <img class= "float-image" src="https://lastfm-img.freetls.fastly.net/i/u/300x300/d95051e07a714889c8f7fbbccf61bf8b.png" alt="Album cover for Example Album" />
  <p>This was always one of those albums that I would always see on critic boards or review site so much so that its cover had become instantly recognisable for me despite not knowing any single off of it. After listening to it I can safely see why so many people rank it amongst the greatest albums of all time.


 
  My first proper experience with the album was having the lead single "Holland 1945" crop up in my shuffle play where I was immediatley hooked. I found it extremely impressive how the guitar is compressed so heavliy without sounding overblown alongside a strong brass section. Considering the album is mostly folk music this harsh sound shouldn't work as well as it does. Besides the main song there are plenty of other songs to keep you hooked as listening through its hard to know where this album is going to go next with each track whether it be in terms of songwriting, lyricism or just raw sound.
  Other highlights on the album would be the title track "In the Aeroplane Over the Sea" and "Two-Headed Boy Pt2" as both are equally beautiful acoustic ballads </p>
</div>

<div class="paragraph-gradient TNA">

#### The Strokes - The New Abnormal

<img class= "float-image" src="https://lastfm-img.freetls.fastly.net/i/u/300x300/576554c542da76c08f0e80c129afcb0e.png" alt="Album cover for Example Album" />
  <p> I've always enjoyed The Strokes since I heard their debut album "Is This It?" and was excited to hear they started to make a comeback in early 2020. "The New Abnormal" serves as a pleasant return to form for the band after what I consider to be a mixed output throughout the 2010s.
  
  
  This record truly feels like the band fully understands what previously worked for them and fully improved and expanded upon it, taking their old indie/garage sound and mixing it with a more electronic one. I couldn't think of a better opening track to demonstrate this point than "The Adults are Talking" a song that soon became one of their concert staples not too long after. The record isn't afraid to show its melancholic side with songs such as "Ode To the Mets" which I consider to be one of the best songs Julian Casablancas has written to date. Another track I suggest you check out is "Bad Decisions" another personal favourite of mine. </p>

</div>

<div class="paragraph-gradient Oasis">

#### Oasis - Definetly Maybe

 <img class= "float-image" src="https://lastfm-img.freetls.fastly.net/i/u/300x300/97874edc19694d7e8d42d3e30918dea6.png" alt="Album cover for Example Album" />
 <p> I feel like Oasis is one of those bands that almost everyone knows a song from, probably due to the immense amount of people who feel the need to play their songs on the nearest acoustic guitar in their immediate vicinty. Nonetheless here is the point from where they exploded onto the britpop scene. Anyway, here's (not) Wonderwall

 I feel it's no surprise how Oasis blew up near instantly from this album. Every song becomes incredibly infectious the more and more I listen to it and it clear that
 this sentiment was shared by many after their singles such as "Supersonic" and "Live Forever". It's the kind of music that can have a crowd practically yelling the lyrics alongside. The absolute peak of this album for me though is "Slide Away". I fell in love with it the first time I heard it and to date is in my opinion the best song the Gallagher brothers have put out to date. "Definetly Maybe" ends up placing itself as a pillar in britpop and a must-hear in my blog. </p>

 </div>
